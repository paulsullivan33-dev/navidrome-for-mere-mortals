# The Neighbours

**Scope:** the honest map of what else
could stream your music — including
doing nothing new at all — with the
same standard applied to all of them:
what each one is for, and when
Navidrome is the wrong answer.

## The option nobody prices: a folder and a player

Before any server: your music already
plays, on the machine it sits on, in
any desktop player (VLC, foobar2000,
Strawberry, a dozen more). If listening
happens at one desk, a server solves a
problem you don't have, and chapter 1's
enthusiasm should not talk you into
one. Navidrome earns its place when
the music must follow you — to phones,
to other rooms, to other people's
accounts — without copying files
around by hand.

## The commercial clouds

Storing the collection in iCloud,
Google Drive, or a streaming service's
"your library" feature outsources
chapter 11 entirely and chapter 3
mostly: no server, no backups, no tags
discipline (they will cheerfully show
you their own metadata instead of
yours, which is either a relief or an
affront depending on your collection).
The prices are rent, the collections
can be re-matched, re-encoded, or
delisted by strangers' business
decisions, and none of it speaks
Subsonic. For a small collection and a
listener who values never thinking
about it, the rent can be honest
value. It is the anti-Navidrome in
every particular, which is exactly why
it suits some people.

## The other self-hosted servers

Navidrome's shelf-mates, fairly
described (none were run on this
bench; this is the map, not a race):

- **Jellyfin** — the cinema of the
  group: video first, music a capable
  second. If the same server must do
  films, Jellyfin is the answer and
  this series has a guide to it.
  Navidrome's very existence is the
  argument that a music-only tool is
  lighter and sharper at music.
  (Notably, Navidrome even carries an
  experimental *OpenSubsonic* extension
  to answer some Jellyfin-client
  calls — neighbours borrowing sugar.)
- **Airsonic / Subsonic itself** —
  Navidrome's ancestors in the protocol.
  The original Subsonic is closed and
  paid; Airsonic is its open descendant,
  heavier (a Java application) and
  slower-moving. Navidrome is the
  modern reimplementation most new
  installs choose.
- **Funkwhale** — the federated one:
  podcasts, sharing between servers,
  a social shape. Choose it when the
  point is a community, not a house.
- **Plex** (with Plexamp) — the
  polished commercial neighbour: lovely
  apps, a company in the middle, an
  account with them required. Many
  Navidrome households are ex-Plex
  households; the reverse commute also
  exists, and neither direction is a
  moral failing.
- **A plain DLNA server** (minidlna
  and friends) — no accounts, no apps,
  no playlists worth the name: it
  announces files to the TV. For one
  smart speaker and no ambitions, it
  is the right size of answer.

## Choosing, in one breath

One house, owned files, phones and
browsers, accounts per listener, and a
weekend's setup: Navidrome. Films too:
Jellyfin. No server tolerated: rent a
cloud. A community across servers:
Funkwhale. The guide you're reading
picked its horse and measured it
honestly (criteria.md keeps the
receipts); this chapter's job is to
make sure the horse was yours to pick.

**Check:** you should be able to say
which neighbour you'd move to if
Navidrome vanished tomorrow, and what
you'd lose in the move (in this
guide's case: the small footprint and
the Subsonic app shelf). If you can,
you chose deliberately — the state
every chapter here has been steering
toward.
